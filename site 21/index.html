The Idea

A pitching coach looks at a pitch's spin, break, and velocity and asks: "should this pitch be good?" Then they watch what actually happens in games and ask: "is it living up to that?" This project builds that same process into a real, data-backed system — four separate models checking four different things a pitch can do (miss bats, avoid hard contact, induce ground balls, and avoid true "barreled" contact), a location layer checking command, and one composite grade tying it all together.

All of it runs on real Major League Baseball data — over 155,000 pitches pulled from Statcast, MLB's public pitch-tracking system, using the open-source pybaseball library. That data teaches the models what a pitch's shape should typically produce; the results are then compared against real pitchers' actual outcomes to find where they beat or miss expectations.
