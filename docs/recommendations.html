Self-Correction

The Changeup's Edge Comes From Command, Not Tunneling

An early diagnosis guessed Skenes' changeup overperformed because it "tunnels" off his fastball — meaning the two pitches look identical out of his hand, making the changeup harder to pick up. Testing this directly (comparing release point and speed between every pair of his pitches) showed the opposite: the changeup–fastball pair is actually one of the worst-matched pairs in his entire arsenal. His true best-tunneling pair is fastball–splitter.

The real explanation for the changeup's dominance is command (see Validation page), not deception. Correcting an assumption when the data disagrees is a stronger sign of good analysis than being right on the first guess.

Honest Limitations

Hard-hit model (AUC 0.546) is the weakest of the four — contact quality has more inherent randomness (defense, luck) than swing-and-miss or batted-ball angle.

Tunneling analysis uses release-point proximity as a stand-in for true pitch-flight similarity, since frame-by-frame tracking data wasn't available.

League baselines are built on one month of 2024 data; a full season would produce more stable numbers.

Hendricks' real decline points to factors (pitch recognition, reduced deception) this tool's current features can't directly measure.

Addressed: small-sample results are now automatically flagged with a confidence label rather than reported at face value.
